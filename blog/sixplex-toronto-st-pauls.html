Can You Build a Sixplex in Toronto–St. Paul's? Ward 12

Toronto–St. Paul's (Ward 12) is one of the nine Toronto wards where up to six residential units are permitted as-of-right, under Zoning By-law 654-2025 and Official Plan Amendment 818 (June 2025). Being in the ward gets a lot to the starting line. Whether six units actually fit — and whether the project pencils — is decided by the specific lot, not the ward.

Updated August 2026 · MBL Developments · one of a nine-part series, one post per ward.

What the ward covers

A midtown ward bounded roughly by Eglinton Avenue to the north, Yonge Street to the east (stepping down toward St Clair Avenue East), the CPR rail corridor near Dupont Street to the south, and Dufferin Street/Oakwood Avenue/Rogers Road to the west. St Clair Avenue West runs across its middle. The ward takes in midtown neighbourhoods including Yonge-Eglinton (south portion) and North Toronto, Casa Loma, South Hill and Rathnelly, Wychwood and Hillcrest-Bracondale, Humewood-Cedarvale, Oakwood-Vaughan and Fairbank, plus portions of Forest Hill and Chaplin Estates/Deer Park. Ward lines follow specific streets and get redrawn from time to time, so treat any neighbourhood list as a guide, not a survey — the City publishes the official ward boundaries on toronto.ca.

Neighbourhoods & built form

The character changes street to street, and the built form is what matters most for a sixplex — it’s the raw material you’re working with, and the heritage-character pockets are where the “does it fit” question gets hardest.

  • Yonge-Eglinton / North Toronto. Older detached and semi-detached houses on side streets, with apartment and condominium buildings concentrated along Yonge Street and Eglinton Avenue.
  • Casa Loma / South Hill. Large older detached homes, some estate-scale, on generous lots near the escarpment and Nordheimer ravine.
  • Wychwood / Hillcrest-Bracondale. Early-20th-century detached and semi-detached houses; Wychwood Park is a designated heritage enclave.
  • Humewood-Cedarvale. Detached and semi-detached houses bordering the Cedarvale ravine and park.
  • Oakwood-Vaughan / Fairbank. Modest detached and semi-detached houses on narrower lots along and north of St Clair Avenue West.
  • Forest Hill (portion) / Chaplin Estates. Larger detached homes on wider lots in the ward's eastern and central sections.

Getting around

Line 1 (Yonge-University) subway serves the eastern edge at Davisville, Eglinton and St Clair stations, with St Clair West station just outside the western boundary. The Eglinton Crosstown LRT (Line 5) runs along the ward's northern edge with stations including Eglinton (Line 1 interchange), Avenue, Chaplin, Forest Hill and Cedarvale. The 512 St Clair streetcar runs in a dedicated right-of-way across the ward along St Clair Avenue West.

Downtown access & lifestyle

A midtown location with subway and streetcar access to downtown. Amenities include the St Clair West retail strip, Artscape Wychwood Barns (home to The Stop's year-round Saturday farmers' market), the Yonge-Eglinton shopping and office node, and ravine green space at Cedarvale and Nordheimer. TD Salsa on St. Clair, a free two-day Latin street festival, runs annually in July along St Clair Avenue West. The mix of transit, retail and parkland supports steady rental demand, consistent with the ward's 61% renter share.

Schools & families

Public high schools serving the area include North Toronto Collegiate Institute at 17 Broadway Avenue in Yonge-Eglinton (TDSB) and Oakwood Collegiate Institute at 991 St Clair Avenue West (TDSB), both long-established TDSB secondary schools. Specific current EQAO scores were not verified from an official source in this pass and should be confirmed against EQAO/TDSB data; families should check the catchment for any given address.

Who lives here

Census figures point to a mix of professionals and smaller households alongside families. The largest age cohort is 30-to-39 at 20.1% (Toronto 16.6%), while children aged 0-9 are 7.8% (below the Toronto average of 9.1%) and residents in their 20s (20-29) are 14.7% (Toronto 15.5%). Seniors (65+) make up 19% versus 17% citywide. The demographic dependency ratio is 44 per 100 working-age residents, matching Toronto.

What that means for a sixplex — and what it doesn’t

A ward that is mostly ground-related, older housing is, in the abstract, the kind of place the six-unit framework was written for: detached, semi-detached, and row houses on residential lots. That is the raw material. It is also exactly where the real questions begin, because none of the ward-level numbers above can tell you what a specific lot will carry.

Being in Ward 12 means the six-unit framework may apply to a lot here. It does not mean six units fit, or that the building is otherwise compliant or pencils. The same lot-by-lot gates apply here as everywhere:

  • Lot geometry. Frontage and depth, minus setbacks, set the buildable envelope. On a narrow lot the sixth unit often has to become a rear suite — a different legal and financing object than six units in one building.
  • Overlays, heritage, and specific zoning. The ward is the boundary; the actual zoning, heritage designations, and overlays run lot by lot and can quietly cap what you build.
  • Trees, easements, servicing. A protected tree, a registered easement, or a servicing constraint can constrain the design, add cost, or reduce the unit count — long before price is the issue.
  • What’s in the ground. On any lot with an existing or former structure, environmental status is unknown until it’s investigated. Real cost, real gate.

A qualified zoning review on the specific lot is step one, and if the review and the ward map ever disagree, the review wins.

How you’d actually evaluate a lot here

The honest sequence is the same in every one of the nine wards: confirm the lot can carry six units on paper (geometry), confirm the market supports the rents (closed-lease evidence, not asking prices), and confirm the numbers pencil at a price you can actually pay — before you fall in love with an address. It runs lot by lot, not ward by ward. That discipline is the whole difference between a buildable sixplex and an expensive lesson.
