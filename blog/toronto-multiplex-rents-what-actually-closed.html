Toronto Rent Benchmark for Multiplex Underwriting — What Actually Closed (August 2026)

The short answer: across 1,846 usable closed 2026 leases spanning 18 Toronto MLS districts, the city-wide medians are $1,850 for 1-bedrooms (n=572), $2,925 for 2-bedrooms (n=680), and $4,100 for 3-bedrooms (n=481) — and in every pocket we track, the median 3-bedroom rents $700–1,700/month above the median 2-bedroom. These are closed leases — what tenants actually signed — not asking prices. To our knowledge, nobody else publishes this table. Note: this is a general-rental benchmark used for multiplex underwriting — the sample includes all rental stock types, not multiplex units only.

Two ways to read the premium, and both matter: the all-stock median gap above partly reflects unit mix — 3-bed listings skew toward houses and upper floors while 2-beds include basements and condos. In the like-for-like comparisons we run in underwriting (similar floor plates, similar condition, same pocket), closed leases still show a 3-bed earning $300–400+/month over a comparable 2-bed — a matched-pair exhibit is planned for a future release of this benchmark. Either way you cut it, family-sized rentals are among the most under-supplied products in the city — see our 3-Bedroom Premium analysis.

Methodology

  • Source: licensed MLS records of closed leases (never asking prices), 2026 year-to-date, across 18 Toronto MLS districts (C01–C03, C08–C10, C13, E01–E04, E06, W01–W03, W06–W08) covering the nine as-of-right sixplex wards and adjacent areas.
  • Sample reconciliation: 1,949 raw records → 1,846 usable after excluding records with missing price/bedroom data or prices outside the $800–$12,000 band (103 excluded). Pocket tables show cells with n ≥ 10; the city-wide rows include all usable records.
  • Cleaning: records missing price or bedroom count excluded; prices outside $800–$12,000/month excluded as data errors or non-arm's-length; bedroom count taken as the primary count (a "3+1" counts as 3).
  • Aggregation: we publish pocket-level aggregates only (n, median, quartiles) — never unit-level records. Pockets shown where n ≥ 10 per unit type.
  • Refresh: monthly. Figures shift as new closes land.

What would make this wrong

  • Condition and size are not controlled. A pocket's median blends renovated and dated units, big and small. Use the quartiles as the honest range, not the median as a promise.
  • Small samples in some cells. A pocket-month with n=10 moves around; the city-wide rows are the most stable.
  • New purpose-built units are a small share of observations. Most closes are older stock; in our experience new purpose-built prints at or above the older-stock band for the same unit type — which makes this benchmark conservative for new construction.
  • A snapshot, not a forecast. This is what closed; markets move.

Frequently asked questions

Why do closed leases matter more than asking prices? An asking price is an opening position. A closed lease is a fact. The gap between the two is where most underwriting goes wrong.

Can I get the underlying data? We publish aggregates monthly. Researchers and journalists can contact us for expanded tables — we don't share unit-level records.

Why publish this at all? Because the market prices what it can see, and almost nobody can see closes. We underwrite with this data either way — publishing the aggregates makes the market smarter and, frankly, proves the machine exists.
