CMHC MLI Select, Explained Like a Human: How Small Builders Get 95% Financing and 50-Year Amortization

The short answer: MLI Select is a CMHC insurance program that finances purpose-built rentals (5+ units) at up to 95% of project cost with amortization up to 50 years — if your project scores 100+ points across affordability, energy efficiency, and accessibility. It is the single most powerful financing tool available to small developers in Canada, and most people who could use it have never read it.

Why this program changes the math

Conventional construction financing runs 65–75% of cost. On a ~$3M sixplex, that's $750k–1M of equity. MLI Select at the 100-point tier allows financing up to 95% of eligible cost — but treat 95% as a ceiling, not a promise: actual loan sizing also runs through the debt-coverage floor, the lender's eligible-cost definitions and full underwriting of the project and borrower group. Real cash requirements land well above "5%" once land closing costs, the insurance premium and PST, carry, and timing are counted — plan on the equity being a mid-six-figure number on a Toronto sixplex, dramatically less than conventional but not a rounding error. The 50-year amortization at the top tier is what makes stabilized new construction cash-flow — that's why the program exists: Canada wants rental housing built, and this is the subsidy.

At 100+ points the package is: up to 95% loan-to-cost · 50-year amortization · limited recourse · a significant discount on the insurance premium.

Where the points come from

Three categories; you need 100+ combined, and there are only a few realistic routes for a small building:

  • Affordability (the big lever — up to 70 points): commit a share of units to rents at or below the affordability threshold for a defined period. On a six-unit building, one affordable unit can clear the 15% tier — the single highest-value design decision available. Run the math: the "lost" rent on one modest unit buys the entire financing package for the other five.
  • Energy efficiency (the realistic partner — 35+ points): design meaningfully beyond a named baseline. The current new-construction tiers are explicit, and the jump between them is a leap in spec — not a rounding step:
Energy points vs NBC Tier 1 vs NECB Tier 1
20 points 20%+ better 25%+ better
35 points 40%+ better 50%+ better

A spec that is "25% better than code" earns 20 points, not 35 — and a 70 + 20 pathway quietly dies at 90. This is real engineering — energy modelling, envelope-first design, testing — and it's where an experienced energy consultant earns their fee many times over. Verify the thresholds against CMHC's current criteria with a dated source before designing to them (last checked: August 2026). - Accessibility: the third category; at the tiers most small projects target, program-wide visitability requirements apply regardless — design them in from day one.

The winning combination for most sixplexes: affordability + energy = 105+, engineered into the design from the first sketch — not bolted on after.

What people get wrong

  • Treating the affordable unit as a cost. It's the cheapest financing you'll ever buy. Price the trade before you dismiss it.
  • Designing the building first and scoring it later. The points are a design brief, not a checklist. Buildings that score 100+ were drawn to score 100+.
  • Ignoring the premium. CMHC's insurance premium plus PST is real cash at the construction band, partially discounted at high tiers — on a Toronto sixplex, budget $150–190k. It surprises everyone the first time.
  • Assuming the timeline is short. Application to first advance takes months and requires completed drawings. Sequencing the application against your land closing is half the game.

What would make this wrong

  • 95% is a ceiling, not an entitlement. The insured loan is also sized by debt-coverage (CMHC's DCR floor) and full lender underwriting of the project and borrower group — weaker rents or covenant can size the loan below the headline LTC.
  • Program criteria change. Point thresholds, premium schedules and energy attestation rules have all been revised before; verify current criteria at CMHC's MLI Select page before designing to them.
  • Timelines are real. Application requires completed drawings, and approval-to-first-advance takes months — sequence it against your land closing.

Frequently asked questions

Does MLI Select work for a first project? The program doesn't require a track record — but lenders underwrite the team. First-timers get financed by partnering: an experienced delivery partner, an energy consultant with program history, a builder with completed projects. Borrowing a track record is a legitimate, common structure.

What does the affordable unit actually rent for? At or below a threshold tied to median renter income for the area — set at application. It's a real discount to market, on one unit, for a defined commitment period — against 95% financing on the whole building.

Is the 50-year amortization forever? It applies to the insured loan — and it's the lever that makes the numbers work. Combined with rent-control exemption on new construction in Ontario, the stabilized building's economics look nothing like old-stock landlording.
